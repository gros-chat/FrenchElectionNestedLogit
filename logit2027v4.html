What happens to the vote if a party changes its line?

Party positions on the issues French voters worry about, scored from the Chapel Hill Expert Survey where it covers them and coded by hand where it doesn't. A nested logit is estimated on 60 party results in seven national elections. Then you move a party's positions and watch votes shift between parties and abstention, or enter the 2027 manifestos and get a predicted first round of the presidential election.

Party characteristics

Promised fiscal gain by income group

For the income-group model each party gets three scores: how much its tax and transfer promises would raise (10) or cut (0) the disposable income of poor, middle and rich households, 5 being neutral. Manifestos are precise about who pays and who gains (brackets, wealth taxes, VAT, minimum wage), so the direction is reliable; they are not costed by income decile, so the magnitudes are judgement. The last published distributional evaluation (IPP) covers the 2017–2022 term, not the 2022 or 2024 programmes. Edit any score.

CHES variables used: spendvtax (tax cuts vs public services), immigrate_policy, eu_position (1–7, rescaled to 0–10), redistribution (reversed), civlib_laworder, eu_russia (reversed: 10 = invest in defence against Russia). Environment vs climate: the CHES "environment" item is broader than global warming (environmental sustainability versus growth). The 2024 wave adds a separate climate-change-policy item, now its own column; it isn't in the 2019 wave, so 2024 climate scores are carried back to the 2017–2019 elections. Among French parties the two items correlate at 0.95, so national results can't separate their effects: include one or the other in the model. Spending control is anchored on Institut Montaigne's costings of the 2024 legislative programmes (added deficit per year: NFP €179bn → 1, RN €71bn → 3.5, Ensemble €21bn → 6). Support for Israel has no expert-survey measure: it is coded from party statements and votes since October 2023 (e.g. LFI's refusal to call Hamas terrorist; RN and Reconquête opposing recognition of Palestine; Macron's recognition in September 2025) and is the least reliable column. Parties outside CHES (LO/NPA, UDR, UPR/Patriotes, and DLF in 2024) are coded by hand. Ensemble is a weighted mix of Renaissance, MoDem and Horizons; NUPES and NFP mix their parties by number of candidacies.

Votes, 2017–2024

Each national direct election is a market. Shares are of registered voters; abstention, blank and null ballots form the outside option. The Senate is left out because it is elected by local councillors, so it has no voter-level shares.

Official Interior Ministry and Conseil constitutionnel results, first rounds. Candidates or lists without a scored party (Lassalle, Génération.s, UDI in 2019, the Animalist party, regionalists, miscellaneous) are left out of the choice set but kept in the valid-vote denominator. Rows marked check use figures not re-verified against the ministry files.

4

Income groups

Post-election surveys report the vote by household income for three elections. Each election × income group becomes a market, which is what lets coefficients differ between poor, middle and rich voters.

Groups follow Ipsos's brackets of household net monthly income: poor below €1,250, middle €1,250–3,000 (two Ipsos brackets merged), rich above €3,000. "Rich" is therefore roughly the upper 40% of households, not the top few percent: the surveys don't go finer. Parties below 4% nationally are left out of the income markets because the survey's rounded percentages are too coarse for them.

Population weights of the three groups among registered voters (defaults are the Ipsos-implied shares, rounded). They weight group results into national totals and centre the income-specific coefficients.

Shares of valid votes (%) in each group: Ipsos–Sopra Steria (presidential 2022, 6–9 April, n=4,000), Ipsos (European 2024, 6–7 June, n=8,923), Ipsos-Talan (legislative 2024, 27–28 June, n=10,286). Legislative 2024 abstention by income wasn't published in the sources found; it is imputed from the European 2024 pattern, scaled to the official 33.3%.

5

Estimation

Berry's (1994) inversion turns the nested logit into a linear equation. The within-nest share is endogenous, so it is instrumented with the number of rivals in the nest and the sum of their characteristics.

ln sjt − ln s0t = Σk βk xjkt + σ ln sj|g,t + λt + ξjt,    ξjt = Xj + εjt

x are the characteristics from step 2 (centred at 5), λt an election fixed effect that absorbs turnout, σ ∈ [0,1) the within-nest correlation, and ξ the unobserved characteristic. Because positions barely move over time, party fixed effects would absorb the β's; instead the party-specific factor Xj is recovered as the party's average ξ, and εjt is what remains in each election (candidate effects, alliances, campaign).

Adds (x−5)² so that support can peak at an interior position rather than rise without limit.
Far right: RN, Reconquête, UDR, DLF, UPR/Patriotes.

Coefficients

Fit

Observed against fitted ln(sj/s0), leaving out ξ. The gap for each point is that party's unobserved characteristic in that election.

Party-specific factor Xj (average unobserved characteristic)

Positive Xj: the party wins more votes than its positions predict (brand, leader, organisation). Negative: fewer. Per-election residuals εjt show, for example, how a presidential candidate over- or under-performs the party's usual level.

Income-group coefficients

Estimated on the income markets from step 4 with a fixed effect for every party × election: anything a party does that affects all groups alike is absorbed, so only how groups differ is identified. That includes the effect of the fiscal promise made to the voter's own group, because the same party promises different things to different groups.

ln sjtg − ln s0tg − σ̂ ln sj|n,tg = φjt + λtg + Σk Δkg xjk + γ · fiscaljg + ejtg

Caveats

Sixty observations across seven elections is thin: standard errors are wide and several positions are coded by hand. Linear characteristics with a common β mean every voter values, say, tighter immigration control in the same direction; nesting captures the fact that close parties compete harder, but not opposing tastes across electorates. The natural next step is a BLP random-coefficients model on department-level results with demographic interactions (foreign-born share, income, age), which the salience gaps in step 1 call for. First-round presidential votes are partly strategic, which a sincere-choice model ignores. The income model rests on 51 survey cells with rounded percentages; its deviations are imprecise and the fiscal scores are coded judgements.

Reading the prediction

This is a first-round, sincere-vote prediction with national coefficients from seven elections, so the ranges are wide and they understate the true uncertainty: they do not include a change of mood in the electorate as a whole, alliances, or strategic voting for a runoff place. The second round is not predicted: a nested logit fitted on first rounds says nothing reliable about how eliminated candidates' voters split (in 2022 it would have made the RN candidate the favourite of the runoff). Load the 2022 field to see how far the model's positions-plus-party-factor prediction lands from the actual 2022 first round; the gap is the candidate-specific residual ε.
