Turnout: local vs presidential

Electorates don't turn out equally outside presidential elections. If a bloc's voters stay home in European or local elections, its results there understate its presidential strength. Set each bloc's turnout rate by election type; the model converts results into presidential-equivalent shares.

Defaults from Ipsos post-election surveys: regional 2021 first-round abstention by party (RN 69%, left 65%, LREM 57%, MoDem 61%, right 55%); European participation (2024: RN 60%, presidential camp 60%, Reconquête 73%; 2019: left 53%, right 54%); municipal 2026 stated intentions (Institut Terram: LFI 55%, social democrats 65%, moderate right 67%). Cells marked * are assumptions where no survey figure exists. Presidential first-round turnout is assumed equal across blocs.

European 2024 reweighted to presidential turnout

If a bloc's share at an election with turnout tk is vk, its underlying support is proportional to vk/tk. Multiplying by presidential turnout and renormalising gives the presidential-equivalent share. The chart compares that with the 2022 presidential first round and the 2027 poll scenario selected in step 6.

The mobilisation ratio is a bloc's turnout in that election divided by its presidential turnout. Below 1 means the bloc's voters turn out less in that election than in a presidential one, so its results there understate its presidential strength.

3

Estimating transfers

The nested logit is calibrated to how each first-round electorate split in the runoff. Surveys are one source; the results of hundreds of constituencies in both rounds are another. This step estimates the 2017 and 2022 transfer matrices five ways and lets you choose which one the model is fitted to.

The sources disagree most about Mélenchon's voters. Ipsos puts 17% of them with Le Pen in 2022; estimates from constituency results put it at 2–6%, close to what a 2024 polling-station study found. Aggregate methods rest on a homogeneity assumption (the same matrix, up to noise, in every constituency) and push small electorates such as Hamon's or Jadot's to corner values; surveys rest on recalled votes. Neither is ground truth, so the forecast in step 7 is shown under every calibration.

0 = constituency results only; near 1 = the survey. Each survey row gets Dirichlet pseudo-counts of λ/(1−λ) times that electorate's size.
Stored result shown; recomputing solves about 2,000 linear programmes with the in-page simplex. The page pauses for several seconds per election.

HET is the share of runoff votes, across all constituencies, that a single national matrix fails to reproduce: 100·Σi,k|Σj xijpjk − yik| / (2Σy). Lower means the matrix fits the local results better; it doesn't mean it is closer to the truth.

The five matrices

Methods

Survey calibrated to national results. Each survey row is drawn from a Dirichlet centred on the Ipsos figures (effective sample about half the respondents), and each draw is raked by iterative proportional fitting so that the matrix reproduces the national first- and second-round totals exactly. This is the minimum-KL (Johnston–Pattie) adjustment of the survey; the spread of draws gives the 90% bands.

Pj· ~ Dirichlet(mjqj·),   P̃ = argminQ KL(Q ‖ P)   s.t.   Σk XjQjk = Xj,   Σj XjQjk = Yk

Hybrid EM and ecological EM. Each constituency's unknown table is filled in by raking a seed xijpjk to that constituency's two sets of totals (E-step); the national matrix is then re-estimated from the pooled tables plus survey pseudo-counts (M-step). This is the MAP estimate under a multinomial model with a Dirichlet prior centred on the survey, in the family of the Joint-EM and ecolRxC-IPF estimators. With λ = 0 it uses constituency results only.

n̂i·· = IPF(xijpjk; xi·, yi·),    pjk ← (Σi n̂ijk + mjqjk) / (Xj + mj),    mj = λ/(1−λ)·Xj

nslphom (Pavía & Romero, 2024). First the lphom linear programme of Romero et al. (2020) finds the matrix that minimises the total absolute error across departments while matching national totals exactly. Then, ten times, each department gets the matrix closest to the current national one (weighted L1) that fits its own totals exactly, and the national matrix is re-aggregated; the step with the lowest heterogeneity is kept. The LPs are solved by a two-phase simplex written into this page (it reproduces HiGHS to four decimals).

min Σi,k |eik|   s.t.   pjk ≥ 0,   Σk pjk = 1,   Σj Xjpjk = Yk,   Σj xijpjk + eik = yik

On 493 real elections where the true tables are published, nslphom and a carefully tuned hierarchical multinomial-Dirichlet model (Rosen, Jiang, King & Tanner, 2001) misallocated about 10% of voters, the best of the methods compared (Pavía & Romero). The hierarchical Bayesian model is not included here: it needs long MCMC runs with tuning and gave unusable estimates for France at department level. Data: constituency results for both rounds, from the Interior Ministry files as cleaned in the tguerra/presidentielle2022 repository; round-2 counts are rescaled to each constituency's round-1 registered voters. Rows: abstention, blank/null, each candidate above 4%, and the remaining candidates pooled as "Others". Survey rows without a published figure (abstainers, blank/null, Others, parts of the finalists' rows) use weak assumed priors.

4

The model

A nested logit with voter segments located on two ideological axes. The first round pins down each candidate's appeal; the runoff, where most candidates disappear, reveals how voters substitute, and that is what identifies the nest parameters.

Voters and utility

Voters belong to six segments k (one per bloc), sized by the bloc's first-round share. A voter in segment k gets utility from candidate j in round r:

Ukjr = δj − βlr (xk − xj)2 − βanti (ak − aj)2 + 1[r=2]·Bkj + εkj,    Uk0r = 1[r=2]·ω + εk0

x is left–right position, a is distance from the political system (anti-system axis), δj is the candidate's appeal, and option 0 is abstaining or casting a blank or null ballot. The shocks ε follow the nested-logit (GEV) distribution: candidates in the same nest m have correlated shocks, with dissimilarity λm (λ = 1 means no correlation; lower means voters stick to the nest).

Pkj = eImeV0 + Σn eIn · eVkj/λmΣi∈m eVki/λm,    Im = λm ln Σi∈m eVki/λm

From round 1 to round 2

Round 1. Given the other parameters, δ is solved so that predicted shares match official results exactly, Σk wk Pkj(δ) = sj. This is the BLP inversion; it uses Newton steps with the analytical nested-logit Jacobian and falls back to the contraction δ ← δ + λ(ln s − ln ŝ).

Round 2. Each voter keeps the same taste shocks. When eliminated candidates are removed one at a time (smallest first), the voters of the removed candidate move according to exact nested-logit diversion ratios:

Dj→r = [ Pr(S ∖ j) − Pr(S) ] / Pj(S)

Zemmour's voters therefore flow to Le Pen when λFR is low, and to everyone else in proportion when it is 1. Aggregate runoff shares equal the nested-logit probabilities on the two-candidate set; only the attribution to origins depends on the removal order. The runoff also adds:

Barrage Bkj = be·μk: when exactly one finalist is far right, non-far-right voters get a bonus for the other finalist (μC = μR = μIND = 1, μRL = eκRL, μSL = eκSL). Outside shift ωe makes abstaining more or less attractive in the runoff. Fatigue φe is the share of the finalists' own voters who drop out. Returning abstainers: the share τe of first-round abstainers who vote in the runoff is set so official runoff turnout is matched exactly.

Anti-LFI vote (2027 only). No radical-left candidate reached the runoff in 2017 or 2022, so the data can't say how centre and right voters would treat one. Step 7 adds a mirror-image bonus for Mélenchon's opponent among centre, right and independent voters, calibrated either to head-to-head polls or to the 2024 legislative duels.

Estimation

Nonlinear least squares on the transfer cells for 2017 and 2022 (the Ipsos survey by default, or a matrix from step 3), plus each runoff's national result. For every trial parameter vector, δ is re-inverted from first-round results, runoff flows are simulated, and the squared gaps are summed.

Parameters

Standard errors come from the Gauss–Newton approximation σ²(J′J)−1 with the delta method. The moments are survey percentages, not independent draws, so treat them as a guide to which parameters the data pin down.

Runoff simulator

Choose two candidates. The model removes everyone else, moves their voters with the estimated diversion ratios, and reports the runoff. You can override those transfers electorate by electorate in the table at the bottom of this step. The win probability comes from Monte Carlo draws over first-round shares, barrage strength and polling error.

Every pairing

Chance that the row candidate beats the column candidate, if both qualify, under the current assumptions. Each cell uses the central model share (blended with polls where available) and a normal approximation with the polling error plus the model spread from step 7.

Caveats

Win probabilities are conditional on the pair reaching the runoff and are this model's calculation, not a published forecast. The transfer moments are survey estimates with their own error. Segment sizes equal bloc first-round shares by assumption. Candidates inherit their bloc's position; Philippe and Attal differ only through δ. The 2026 Senate figures are provisional. Polls seven months out have historically missed by several points, and the 2017 field changed substantially after September 2016.
